# Financial Scenario Methodology

The project evaluates the energy-efficiency investment under three analytical scenarios.

These scenarios are designed to test how financing assumptions and energy-cost escalation affect project value.

## Scenario Assumptions

| Scenario | Discount Rate | Analysis Horizon | Energy Price Escalation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| LOW | 4% | 20 years | 2% |
| BASE | 6% | 20 years | 0% |
| HIGH | 8% | 20 years | 0% |

These are **analyst-defined assumptions** for scenario testing.

They should not be interpreted as UWSP financing terms.

## Project Inputs

The modeled energy-conservation program includes:

- First cost after incentives: approximately **$8.9 million**
- Annual energy cost savings: approximately **$607,000**
- Simple payback: approximately **14.66 years**

## NPV Results

| Scenario | NPV |
|---|---:|
| LOW | +$867,632 |
| BASE | -$1,937,758 |
| HIGH | -$2,940,385 |

## Interpretation

The project economics are highly sensitive to financing assumptions and future energy-cost conditions.

The LOW scenario produces positive value because it combines a lower discount rate with energy-price escalation.

The BASE and HIGH scenarios remain financially challenged.

This demonstrates why a single deterministic NPV is not sufficient for energy-transition decision-making.

## Decision Use

The scenario analysis supports questions such as:

- How sensitive is project value to the discount rate?
- How much does energy-price escalation affect the investment case?
- Under what conditions does the project become value-creating?
- When might climate, operational, or resilience benefits justify investment even if financial returns are weak?
