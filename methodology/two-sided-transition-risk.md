\# Two-Sided Transition Risk



The project originally treated transition risk mainly as exposure to purchased EACs and incomplete renewable-attribute evidence.



The model was later extended to recognize that transition risk can move in more than one direction.



\## Scenario Design



Four analyst-defined scenarios are used:



| Scenario | Interpretation |

|---|---|

| POLICY\_SUPPORT | Supportive climate policy and financing conditions |

| CURRENT\_PATH | Baseline analytical case |

| GREEN\_HEADWIND | Higher financing costs and weaker renewable-policy support |

| CARBON\_CONSTRAINT | Stronger carbon policy and higher conventional-energy exposure |



These are stress scenarios, not UWSP forecasts.



\## Risk Channels



The model separates:



\- efficiency financial resilience

\- solar policy exposure

\- purchased EAC exposure

\- transition-adjusted NPV



\## Key Principle



A green investment is not automatically low-risk.



Renewable projects can also face downside from:



\- higher financing costs

\- weaker subsidies

\- policy changes

\- market shifts

\- lower-than-expected value of environmental attributes



The purpose of the scenario layer is to test how the portfolio behaves under both supportive and adverse transition conditions.
